# Genesis Pool

A self-running evolution simulation. You only watch.

Live: https://nathansix.github.io/genesis-pool/

## What evolves

- **Bodies** are chains of sections. Sections can be copied and repurposed as fins, legs, eyes, jaws, shells, spines, fat stores or feelers, so body plans grow and diversify over time.
- **Brains** are small neural networks that can add neurons by duplication, gain short-term memory, and send and read signals (flashes) between members of a species.
- **Learning**: each animal is born with a slightly imperfect copy of its inherited brain and can tune it during its life. Connections that were active just before a better-than-usual meal get stronger; connections active before getting hurt get weaker. How fast an animal learns is itself a gene.
- **Reproduction** is sexual: ready animals seek a mate of their own species, and offspring mix both parents' genes. An animal that finds no partner for a long time reproduces alone.
- **Plants** are organisms too, evolving growth rate, toughness, dispersal, temperature preference and tolerance for dry land.

## What changes around them

Day turns to night (eyes work worse in the dark, feelers don't, and algae grow more slowly), seasons shift the warm and cold bands north and south, and blooms of food come and go. Algae slowly fill the water with oxygen, which sets how large animals can grow. Later the seas recede and land rises. Climate swings, asteroid strikes, supervolcanoes and plagues on overcrowded species reshuffle the living world. If animal life crashes, the most complex recent lineage returns from a hidden refuge instead of evolution restarting from scratch. The full arc takes roughly 4 billion simulated years (about 30 minutes at normal pace), and complexity keeps climbing slowly after that.

## Notes

- Everything runs in the browser from one file, `index.html`.
- Each browser keeps its own world and autosaves it. When you return, it catches up on up to about 2 minutes of evolution.
- The "Do bigger brains help?" chart plots every living species by brain size against how much food it gathers for what it costs to stay alive.
- Double-click (or use Zoom) to zoom in, drag to look around, and click any animal to see its body parts and a live diagram of its brain.
